## Thesis

The passage argues that recording assumptions and evidential limits necessarily
improves every research decision.

## Argument Map

1. Structured prompts can expose assumptions and limits.
2. Exposing those distinctions can support better review.
3. Implicit warrant: better visibility always produces better decisions.
4. Therefore, the proposed tool necessarily improves every research decision.

## Evidence

The passage supplies a plausible mechanism but no observed comparison showing
improved decisions.

## Assumptions

- Researchers notice and correctly use the recorded distinctions.
- Added structure does not create enough friction to offset its benefit.

## Counterarguments

Inference: visibility is useful only when users interpret and act on it well;
recording a distinction alone does not guarantee a better decision.

## Boundaries

The passage supports testing the tool's effect on judgment. It does not establish
universal or necessary improvement.

## Implications

The argument justifies a small comparison with a defined decision-quality
criterion before committing to a larger build.
